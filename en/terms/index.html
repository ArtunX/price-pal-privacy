3. What PricePal does

  • Discount: given a price and a discount percentage above 0 and up to 100, it calculates the discounted price and the saving. If you add a second discount, it applies it to the price already discounted.
  • Tip: given the amount of a bill and a percentage, it calculates the tip and the total. It can add sales tax to the bill, at the rate you type, and work out the tip on the amount before or after tax.
  • Splitting the bill: it divides the total among several people. If the total does not divide into equal parts, some shares are one unit higher (for the euro, one cent). If you ask, it rounds up the total or each person's share to the step you choose, and what the rounding adds goes to the tip.
  • VAT and sales tax: given an amount and a rate, it adds the tax or takes it out of a price that includes it, and shows the net amount, the tax and the total.
  • Unit price: given the price and quantity of several packs, it calculates the price per unit of measure (for example per kg, per liter or per piece) and shows which one costs less. It does not compare packs measured by weight with packs measured by volume or by pieces.
  • Rounding: amounts are rounded to the decimal places of the chosen currency (for the euro, to the cent; for the yen, to the unit).
  • Currency: choosing a currency changes the symbol and the number of decimal places. The app does not convert amounts from one currency to another.
  • What the app does not know: it does not know which taxes, charges, promotions or rounding the shop or venue applies. The amount to pay is the one they ask you for.
  • Suggested percentages: the suggested discount, tip and VAT percentages are shortcuts you can change. The tip ones do not indicate how much it is customary to tip. The VAT ones are rates of the country you choose, as of the date the app shows, and may not be the ones that apply to your purchase.
  • History: it keeps on your phone the calculations you save, at most 5,000: when you save one more, the oldest is deleted. You can share or copy a calculation, export the history as a spreadsheet (CSV) and save a backup of it in a file, from which you can restore it together with the percentages and the settings.
